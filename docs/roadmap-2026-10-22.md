# Publication dashboard enrichment roadmap

Date: 22 October 2026. Scope: the publications dashboard and its pipeline. This
builds on the [September review](repository-review-2026-09-12.md) and the
[implementation record](implementation-2026-09-12.md); it does not revisit their
findings.

The aim is richer, more consistent data about ZMO's publications, and views
that show patterns in theme and time rather than only counts. Every addition
keeps the existing contract: data is scraped or fetched, never maintained by
hand outside the curated files; every derived value is reproducible and
labelled with where it came from; and an absence in a third-party source is
never presented as an absence in reality.

## Principles

1. **Say where a value came from, on the page.** OpenAlex, zmo.de, the
   embedding model and the language model each get a visible attribution next
   to what they produced, not only in the README.
2. **Coverage before conclusions.** Each enriched view states its denominator:
   "322 of 1,976 publications matched in OpenAlex", not a chart that silently
   covers a sixth of the register.
3. **No rankings of people by citations.** Citation data in this field is thin
   and skewed towards English-language journal articles. It is used to show
   which literature ZMO draws on and how its own works cite each other, never
   to rank researchers.
4. **AI output is labelled as such.** Theme labels are generated by Claude Opus
   5.5 (Anthropic) and say so wherever they appear. The evidence each label was
   written from is committed beside it.
5. **No personal data beyond what the page already shows publicly.** People
   pages contribute a name, a URL, a position, research units and an ORCID iD if
   one is linked. No email addresses or phone numbers. Student assistants are
   not collected.
6. **Byte-identical when nothing changed.** No timestamps in generated files;
   deterministic ordering and seeds everywhere.

## Phases

Status key: ☐ not started · ◐ in progress · ☑ done.

### Phase 1 — Researcher pages ☑

*Question: who among the register's authors is (or was) at ZMO, and where can a
reader find out more about them?*

- `data_prep/scrape_people.py` crawls the people listings (directorate, research
  fellows, associated, affiliated, guests, research support, former fellows and
  staff) and each person's page into `data_prep/raw_data/people.json`.
- The generator matches register authors to people with the same name rules it
  already uses for consolidation (case, accents, name order, unique given-name
  prefix). Ambiguous matches are refused, as they are for merges.
- Dashboard: author names in the publication list link to their zmo.de page; an
  author filter shows a profile line (position, research units, zmo.de, ORCID);
  the co-authorship network distinguishes ZMO people from outside co-authors.

Limits to state: research units are *current* memberships (read only from the
"current research" lines of a page) and are shown on the person, never
back-projected onto their older publications.

*Done.* The baseline above underestimated the directory: every listing is
paginated at ten people per page, and former fellows and staff run to 21 pages
(206 people). 290 people in all; 174 register authors linked, appearing in
1,718 of 1,976 publications; 53 of the 84 current people have a research unit.

### Phase 2 — Facets from the current data ☑

- **Language of publication**, detected from title and subtitle with
  [Lingua](https://github.com/pemistahl/lingua-py) restricted to the languages
  the register uses, with an explicit "undetermined" bucket for short or mixed
  titles (transliterated Arabic titles with an English translation are common).
  Labelled as automatic detection.
- **Open access**, from OpenAlex (Phase 3) for matched records; "unknown"
  for the rest, never "closed".
- Language share over time in *Explore patterns*.

*Done.* Lingua alone mislabelled titles in four systematic ways (bracketed
translations, Arabic script with a transliteration, transliterated Arabic,
place names outvoting grammar), so function words decide first and Lingua is
consulted only when they are inconclusive. Book reviews are undetermined: the
title is the reviewed book's. Result: 1,157 English, 343 German, 79 French, 22
Arabic, 15 Turkish, a dozen in other languages, 338 undetermined (163 of them
reviews).

### Phase 3 — OpenAlex enrichment ☑

- `data_prep/enrich_openalex.py` matches register records to OpenAlex: by DOI
  first, then by title with year (±1) and author-surname checks for records
  without one. Writes `data_prep/raw_data/openalex.json`.
- Per matched work: OpenAlex id, match method, OA status and URL, language,
  primary topic, institutions (with country), authors with ORCID iDs, funders
  and referenced works. OpenAlex data is CC0. Abstracts are **not** copied: they
  belong to the publishers, and the register's own abstracts suffice.
- Referenced works cited by at least two register publications are resolved to
  titles, for an "intellectual base" view. ZMO-to-ZMO citations are kept as
  edges between register records.
- A gap report, `data_prep/reports/openalex-gaps.csv`, lists ZMO-affiliated
  OpenAlex works that match nothing in the register, and register records whose
  DOI OpenAlex does not know. It is for ZMO's library and is not deployed.
- Dashboard: collaborating institutions and countries (from co-authors'
  affiliations); OA status as a facet and a badge; and a **citations view**
  headed by its caveat — *Based on OpenAlex. Only N of 1,976 publications could be
  matched and only M of those have reference lists; books, chapters and
  non-English publications are poorly covered. A missing link is not a missing
  citation.*

*Done.* 833 of 1,976 matched (358 by DOI, 143 via ZMO's affiliated works, 332 by
title); 322 with a reference list; 226 citations between register works; 1,202
works cited by at least two. OpenAlex now meters requests, so title searches
are batched and the monthly run is incremental. Works without authors are
accepted only for books and chapters, after the first run showed anonymous
reviews and namesakes slipping through.

### Phase 4 — Thematic map ☑

*Question: what does ZMO publish about, and how has that changed?*

- **Embeddings.** [`google/embeddinggemma-2`](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2)
  (Apache 2.0, multilingual, 768 dimensions), run through its pinned ONNX export
  in fp32 with onnxruntime — the same path, revisions and settings as the IWAC
  pipeline, so no torch and no API key. Input: the clustering task prefix, then
  title, subtitle and, where the register has one, the abstract. Multilingual
  embeddings put German, English and French titles on the same subject in the
  same neighbourhood, so language does not split themes.
- **Clusters.** Fine-grained themes (around 20–30) from the embeddings, grouped
  into at most eight broad areas so the map can use the site's eight-colour
  categorical palette. Fixed seeds; parameters recorded in the output.
- **Map.** A 2-D UMAP projection (cosine metric, fixed seed) of every record.
- **Labels.** English and German labels and one-sentence descriptions for each
  theme and area, **written by Claude Opus 5.5** from a committed brief
  (`data_prep/reports/theme-brief.md`: each cluster's distinctive terms and
  representative titles). Stored in `data_prep/theme_labels.json` with the model
  id, date and method, and shown with that attribution in the dashboard.
- **Stability.** A full re-cluster is a deliberate, local operation followed by
  relabelling. Between re-clusters, new records are assigned to the nearest
  theme and placed beside their nearest neighbours on the map, by a workflow that
  runs after the monthly publication refresh. Labels carry a signature of the
  membership they were written for, so a stale label is detectable.
- Dashboard: the map (hover a point for its title, click to open it, click a
  theme to filter), a theme ranking that cross-filters like every other chart,
  and themes over time.

*Done, with three decisions taken on evidence:*

- **Titles only, no abstracts.** With abstracts appended, clusters split by
  whether a record had one (0–99% abstract share per cluster).
- **Context only from a containing volume.** Journal and series names as
  context for short titles turned genres — encyclopaedia entries, a project's
  online resources — into themes.
- **Language centring.** Subtracting each language's mean vector (detected on
  the embedded text) broke up a German-language and a French-language cluster.

24 themes in 8 areas; three themes group short or generic titles and are
labelled "Mixed". Clicking a point filters by its theme rather than opening the
record: the list underneath is one scroll away and opening two thousand
external links from a scatter plot by accident was the worse failure.

### Phase 5 — Regions studied and library subject headings ☑

- Places and regions *studied* (not publishing places) extracted from titles and
  abstracts with a curated multilingual gazetteer linked to Wikidata ids, plus
  each researcher's stated regional focus. Every extraction keeps its evidence
  (the matched string). Regions × time; a map once extraction has been reviewed.
  The September review deferred this until extraction could be checked; that
  check is the work of this phase.
- GND subject headings for monographs and edited volumes with an ISBN
  (245 of 317), from the German National Library / lobid. These are assigned by
  librarians and fill the gap where OpenAlex is weakest.

*Done.* A 206-place gazetteer in three languages, every Wikidata id verified
(35 corrected) and coordinates fetched; places matched in titles, subtitles and
abstracts: 1,367 of 1,979 publications name one. All 46 matches of the five
ambiguous names were reviewed record by record (3 rejected) and a sample per
region read; the review is labelled as Claude Opus 5.5's, not ZMO's. GND
headings for 133 of 245 books with an ISBN, from lobid and the DNB, merged and
checked against the register's titles. Dashboard: a world map with a place
ranking and region legend, researchers' stated regional focus, regions over
time, a subject-heading ranking, and place and heading tags in the list. Also
added on request: a year slider, author timelines as a heatmap, full-screen
panels, and a reviewer field for the theme names.

## Data contracts

Pipeline inputs and outputs added by this roadmap. All JSON is UTF-8, sorted,
two-space or one-space indented as the existing files are, without timestamps.

```
data_prep/raw_data/people.json      zmo.de people pages            scrape_people.py
data_prep/raw_data/openalex.json    OpenAlex matches and citations  enrich_openalex.py
data_prep/raw_data/themes.json      embeddings → clusters + map     build_themes.py
data_prep/theme_labels.json         theme labels (Claude Opus 5.5)  written from reports/theme-brief.md
data_prep/reports/                  gap report, theme brief         not deployed

publications_dashboard/data/publications.json   + lang, theme, oa, openalex, oaUrl per record
publications_dashboard/data/people.json         matched authors → zmo.de profile
publications_dashboard/data/themes.json         labels, areas, map coordinates (lazy)
publications_dashboard/data/openalex.json       institutions, citations, coverage (lazy)
publications_dashboard/data/meta.json           schemaVersion 2, coverage counts
```

New filter dimensions and their URL parameters: `lang` (ISO 639-1 code, `und`
for undetermined), `theme` (theme id), `access` (`open`, `closed`, `unknown`).

## Environments

- `requirements.txt` — the scrapers, generators and CI. Gains
  `lingua-language-detector`.
- `requirements-themes.txt` — embeddings, clustering and projection only
  (onnxruntime, tokenizers, huggingface_hub, numpy, scikit-learn, umap-learn).
  Not installed by Validate or Deploy: the committed `raw_data/themes.json` is
  what the generator reads, so CI never needs the model. The *Update themes*
  workflow installs it after each publication refresh, and only when there is
  something new to place. On Windows on ARM numba crashes inside LLVM; use an
  x64 Python for UMAP.

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Clusters reflect title wording rather than subject | Representative titles in the brief are read before labelling; clusters that are incoherent are labelled as mixed rather than given a confident name |
| Records with abstracts embed differently from title-only ones | Checked by comparing abstract share per cluster; abstract length is capped |
| UMAP output differs across machines | Coordinates are a committed artefact of a deliberate run, not regenerated in CI |
| OpenAlex title matches are wrong | Title similarity, year and surname must all agree; match method is recorded and shown |
| OpenAlex rate limits or API changes | Polite pool with contact address, page-based paging (cursor paging now requires a key), retries; the step fails loudly rather than writing a partial file |
| A people page lists a namesake | Matching requires a unique full-name match; ambiguous names stay unlinked |
